"Hangovers Aren't About How Much You Drank. They're About How Fast One Enzyme Clears The Toxin."

When you drink, your liver converts alcohol into a compound called acetaldehyde, which is 10 to 30 times more toxic than the alcohol itself. Acetaldehyde is the main cause of the symptoms you know.

  • The flush. The racing heartbeat.
  • The nausea.
  • The pounding head.
  • The brain fog. The bone-deep exhaustion the next morning.

None of it is the alcohol. It's the acetaldehyde sitting in your bloodstream as your body couldn't clear it fast. Your body has exactly one enzyme that does that job: ALDH2 (aldehyde dehydrogenase 2). The faster ALDH2 works, the less time acetaldehyde stays in your system. The less time it stays, the better your morning.
